\# MDS-0005 — Combat System



Status: Draft



Owner: FN Productions



\---



\# Purpose



This document defines the combat philosophy of Project KATANA.



It intentionally describes \*\*how combat should feel\*\*, rather than how individual mechanics should be implemented.



Every future combat-related system—including movement, attacks, hitboxes, stamina, enemies, bosses, animations, visual effects, audio, and camera behavior—must align with the principles defined here.



\---



\# Combat Vision



Project KATANA is an action combat game built around mastery.



Combat should reward observation, timing, positioning, and deliberate decision-making rather than repetitive execution or numerical progression.



The objective is not to overwhelm the player with complexity.



The objective is to create encounters that are satisfying to learn and rewarding to overcome.



Victory should always feel earned.



\---



\# Combat Identity



Project KATANA combat should make the player feel calm, confident, constantly engaged, and deeply rewarded, while always maintaining enough tension that every encounter demands respect.



The player should leave every difficult encounter believing:



> "I earned that victory."



\---



\# Core Combat Principles



\## 1. Mastery Over Grinding



Player improvement should matter more than statistical progression.



Learning enemy behavior should provide greater rewards than simply increasing damage values.



Knowledge is progression.



\---



\## 2. Every Decision Matters



Combat should continuously ask meaningful questions.



Examples include:



\- Attack or wait?

\- Dodge or block?

\- Continue a combo or disengage?

\- Spend stamina now or save it?



The correct answer depends on the situation rather than a single dominant strategy.



\---



\## 3. Read Before React



Enemy attacks should be readable.



Difficulty should primarily come from recognizing attack patterns and responding correctly rather than relying on hidden mechanics or unfair reactions.



Players should lose because they made poor decisions—not because information was unavailable.



\---



\## 4. Responsive Controls



Player input should be reflected immediately.



Animation should follow gameplay.



Gameplay must never wait for animation.



Responsiveness always has higher priority than realism.



\---



\## 5. Weight Without Sluggishness



Characters should feel powerful and grounded.



Weight should be communicated through:



\- animation

\- sound

\- camera

\- hit reactions

\- visual effects



Movement should never become slippery or unresponsive.



\---



\## 6. Reward Precision



Players who develop better timing should naturally perform better.



Precise play should provide advantages without making casual play impossible.



Mastery should emerge naturally through experience.



\---



\# Combat Pace



Combat should maintain a medium tempo.



Fights should neither become extremely short nor unnecessarily prolonged.



Mistakes should be meaningful but not instantly fatal.



Recovery should always be possible through skillful play.



\---



\# Offensive Philosophy



Attacking should encourage deliberate decision-making.



Basic attacks should remain accessible to new players.



Experienced players should discover additional depth through timing, spacing, and combo optimization.



Combat should reward intentional inputs over random button mashing.



\---



\# Defensive Philosophy



Defense consists of multiple equally viable options.



\## Dodge



Dodging is the primary defensive tool.



Dodges should provide generous invulnerability frames while still requiring proper timing.



Well-timed dodges should feel satisfying rather than stressful.



\---



\## Block



Blocking is a reliable defensive option.



Blocking consumes stamina.



Certain attacks cannot be blocked and must instead be avoided.



The player should recognize these attacks through clear visual communication.



\---



\## Parry



Parrying is powerful but optional.



Successful parries should provide meaningful rewards.



The game should never require parries to complete encounters.



Players should be free to specialize in dodging, blocking, parrying, or a combination of all three.



\---



\# Stamina Philosophy



Normal movement and basic attacks should remain fluid.



Stamina should primarily regulate defensive actions and advanced combat options.



Examples include:



\- Blocking

\- Dodging

\- Weapon Skills

\- Special abilities



The player should rarely feel prevented from attacking simply because stamina is empty.



\---



\# Lock-On Philosophy



Lock-on is an optional tool.



It is primarily intended for:



\- Boss encounters

\- Elite enemies

\- One-on-one engagements



Players should be free to switch between lock-on and free camera movement at any time.



\---



\# Hit Feel



Every successful hit should communicate impact.



Version 1 focuses on clear visual confirmation.



Future iterations should gradually incorporate:



\- hit reactions

\- hit stop

\- camera feedback

\- particles

\- improved sound

\- visual polish



The long-term objective is for every successful strike to feel physically satisfying.



\---



\# Boss Philosophy



Bosses are the defining moments of Project KATANA.



They should be remembered because they are:



\- visually memorable

\- mechanically engaging

\- emotionally satisfying



Bosses should prioritize spectacle without sacrificing gameplay quality.



Difficulty exists to support the experience—not to become the experience itself.



\---



\# Weapon Philosophy



Players may choose from multiple weapon classes.



However, long-term mastery of a chosen weapon should be encouraged.



Each weapon should develop its own identity rather than simply serving as a statistical upgrade.



\---



\# Fairness



Every failure should be understandable.



Players should always know:



\- why they lost

\- what they could improve

\- how they can perform better next time



Combat should never rely on hidden information or unavoidable damage.



\---



\# Future Scope



This document intentionally avoids implementation details.



Individual systems are specified separately.



Examples include:



\- Combat Controller

\- Hitbox System

\- Attack System

\- Stamina System

\- Lock-On System

\- Enemy AI

\- Boss AI

\- Weapon Framework



These systems must follow the philosophy established by this document.
