\# CDD-0001 — Combat Philosophy



Status: Approved



Owner: FN Productions



\---



\# Purpose



This document defines the combat philosophy of Project KATANA.



It is intentionally independent of implementation details, engine limitations, and specific mechanics.



Every combat system should reinforce the principles described here.



When future design decisions conflict, this document serves as the primary reference.



\---



\# Combat Identity



Project KATANA is a combat-first action game.



Combat is not designed around speed, visual spectacle, or damage numbers.



Combat is designed around rhythm, decision-making, commitment, and mastery.



Every encounter should feel like a conversation between the player and the enemy.



\---



\# Core Principles



\## 1. Every Action Has Intention



Every player action should feel deliberate.



Nothing should happen because of:



\- button mashing

\- luck

\- animation abuse

\- glitches

\- accidental inputs



The player should always feel:



> "I chose that."



\---



\## 2. Flow Over Speed



Combat should feel fluid rather than fast.



Responsiveness is essential, but frantic gameplay is not.



Smooth transitions between movement, attacks, defense, and recovery create satisfying combat.



\---



\## 3. Rhythm Over Reactions



Combat rewards learning rhythm rather than pure reflexes.



Players should recognize attack patterns, timing windows, and opportunities through observation and practice.



Mastery comes from understanding the flow of combat.



\---



\## 4. Commitment Creates Meaning



Actions should carry commitment.



Attacks should not be freely canceled at any moment.



Likewise, players should never feel unfairly locked into long, unavoidable animations.



Well-designed cancel windows create meaningful choices.



\---



\## 5. Risk Must Match Reward



Every offensive option carries an appropriate level of risk.



Low-risk actions produce modest rewards.



High-reward attacks expose the player to greater punishment if used carelessly.



Powerful actions should feel earned.



\---



\## 6. Reading the Enemy



Combat is a dialogue.



Players succeed by understanding enemy behavior rather than overpowering statistics.



Observation should consistently outperform aggression.



\---



\## 7. Fairness Above Difficulty



Failures should feel understandable.



Players should immediately recognize why they were defeated and how they could improve.



Difficulty should emerge from demanding mechanics rather than hidden information or artificial advantages.



\---



\## 8. Mastery Over Grinding



Player improvement should primarily come from skill development.



Progression systems may support combat but should never replace mastery.



Winning because the player became better is always preferred over winning because numbers increased.



\---



\## 9. Animation Supports Gameplay



Animations communicate gameplay.



Animations should never delay, hide, or contradict gameplay decisions.



Gameplay always has priority.



Animation exists to reinforce player feedback.



\---



\## 10. Movement Is Part of Combat



Movement is an offensive and defensive tool.



Positioning, spacing, timing, and distance management are fundamental combat skills.



Standing still should rarely be the optimal strategy.



\---



\## 11. Clarity Before Spectacle



Visual effects should improve readability.



Every attack, dodge, parry, and hit should clearly communicate:



\- timing

\- direction

\- danger

\- recovery



Readability is always more important than visual complexity.



\---



\## 12. Consistency Builds Trust



The same action should produce the same result under identical conditions.



Players should develop confidence in the combat system through consistency.



Predictability enables mastery.



\---



\# Combat Goals



Project KATANA combat should make the player feel:



\- calm

\- focused

\- engaged

\- rewarded

\- constantly attentive



The objective is controlled intensity rather than chaos.



Players should remain comfortable while feeling that every decision matters.



\---



\# What Combat Should Never Become



Combat should never encourage:



\- mindless button mashing

\- unavoidable deaths

\- random outcomes

\- artificial difficulty

\- excessive animation locks

\- repetitive strategies

\- stat-check victories



\---



\# Design Test



Every future combat mechanic should answer the following questions.



Does it reward mastery?



Does it preserve combat rhythm?



Does it encourage intentional decisions?



Does it improve readability?



Does it feel fair?



If the answer to any question is "No", the mechanic should be redesigned before implementation.



\---



\# Long-Term Vision



The goal is to create combat that remains satisfying after hundreds of hours.



Players should continue discovering better timing, spacing, positioning, and strategy rather than relying on larger numbers or stronger equipment.



The combat system should reward understanding.



Mastery should always be the greatest advantage available to the player.
